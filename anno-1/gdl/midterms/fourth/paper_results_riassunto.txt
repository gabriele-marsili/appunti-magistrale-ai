Setup:
- T = 500 diffusion timestamps 
- w = 0.05 for fn C'(t)
- D = T/2 
- v = 1 for node matricies' noise schedulers and v=1.5 for the edge matrices 
- lambda_X = 1, lambda_E = 2
- p_min = 0.2, p_max = 1
- guidance scale lambda = 3 (QM9); = 2 for ZINK-250k

Datasets:
- QM9 : (133k molecules) 9 non hydrogen atoms 
    - TR: first 100000 samples
    - TS: 10% overall data 
    - VL: rest of the data 
- ZINK-250k : (250k) drug-like molecules, removed stereochermistry info and non frequent non-neutrally-charged atoms
    - TR: first 80% of data
    - VL: 10% data 
    - TS: 10% data

Tasks
• Proper targeting 
-> 100 property vectors from test set, 10 molecules for each of them
-> MAE between target properties y and generated molecules y_tilde 
-> comparison against DiGress (GDM for conditional graph, classifier-based guidance) and FreeGress (GDM, classifier-free guidance)

• Optimization
-> objective: generate molecules that improve on a certain property while meeting a
similarity constraint
-> molecule corrupted for 100 steps; vec. y = target value 
    - LogP: 800 molecules, sampled 20 candidates for each of them -> kept only the ones with Tanimoto similarity with the starting molecule >= threshold delta (in {0.4 , 0.6})
    - QED: 800 molecules, sampled 20 candidates for each of them -> 1 candiate with QED between 0.9 and 1.0 and a fingerprint Tanimoto similarity >= 0.4
    - DRD2 : 800 molecules (acrivity score <= 0.05), sampled 20 candidates -> 1 candiate with DRD2 score >= 0.5 and a fingerprint Tanimoto similarity >= 0.4
-> comparison against Junction-Tree Variational Autoencoder, Graph Convolutional Policy
Network and Constrained Graph Variational Autoencoders

• Out-of-distribution sampling:
-> generate valid molecules outside the training distribution 
    -> insert up to 14 nodes ; 15 in generation 

Reults:
- State of art performances, improves in MAE by 14% 
- improved capacity in generating valid molecules without sacrificing the MAE
- smoother tradeoff between validity and accuracy
- Out-of-distribution sampling: 35% of valid molecules with 15 nodes => usable in out-of-distribution sampling, as it is able to learn graph sizes (that do not appear in the training dataset)

Property Optimization
- GRIDDD outperforms all competitors (in both settings in terms of improvement)
- GRIDDD scores remarkably better in the other 2 tasks
- (better than methods that use 3D information to optimize molecules)

Computational considerations:
- overhead due to insertions and deletions during training (30% slower than FreeGress)
- flexibility in adapting the number of denoising steps => faster to sample from 
• Ablation:
    - same experiments with GRIDDD but disabling insertions and deletions
        - success rate remains relatively unchanged in the LogP and DRD2 experiments, it significantly drops to 33.8% when optimizing QED (dependent by molecular weight)



The margin becomes larger on graph-property optimization, where QED success climbs to 45.1% against 9.4% for GCPN, with a LogP
improvement of 2.70. It grows further out of distribution: trained only on molecules up to 14 atoms, GrIDDD still produces 35% valid samples at
size 15, while DiGress collapses to near zero at that scale. An ablation isolates the source of the gain. Switching insertion and deletion off drops
QED success from 45.1% to 33.8%, so the result is carried by size control rather than by the diffusion backbone alone.
